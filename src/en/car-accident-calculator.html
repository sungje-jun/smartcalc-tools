How Car Accident Settlements Work

A car accident settlement compensates you for economic damages (medical bills, lost wages, property damage) and non-economic damages (pain and suffering, emotional distress).

The multiplier method is the most common way to calculate pain and suffering. Insurance adjusters multiply your medical bills by 1.5x to 5x depending on injury severity. More severe injuries with longer recovery times get higher multipliers.

Comparative fault: In most states, your settlement is reduced by your fault percentage. If you're 20% at fault for a $100,000 claim, you'd receive $80,000. Some states bar recovery entirely if you're 50%+ at fault.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I accept the insurance company's first offer?

Almost never. First offers are typically 30-50% lower than fair value. Counter with your calculated amount and be prepared to negotiate. Consider hiring a personal injury attorney for claims over $10,000.

How much does a personal injury lawyer cost?

Most work on contingency: 33% of settlement before trial, 40% if it goes to trial. You pay nothing upfront. Studies show represented claimants receive 3.5x more on average even after attorney fees.

What if the other driver has no insurance?

File under your own Uninsured Motorist (UM) coverage. If you don't have UM coverage, you may need to sue the driver directly, though collecting can be difficult.

When should I settle vs. go to trial?

Settle when the offer is fair and you want faster resolution. Consider trial when liability is clear, damages are high, and the insurer's offer is unreasonably low. Only 4-5% of personal injury cases go to trial.
